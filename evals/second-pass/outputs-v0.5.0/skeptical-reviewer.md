# Skeptical reviewer

## Critical
1. **Discussion — causal mechanism.** The central interpretation moves from baseline association to “mediate protection.” The observational Cox model cannot establish mediation or a protective mechanism. The strongest defensible conclusion is an association with delayed parasitemia.

## Major
2. **Discussion — proposed IFN-gamma mechanism.** No mechanistic or perturbation experiment tested IFN-gamma. This should be framed as a hypothesis rather than an explanation of the observed association.
3. **Discussion — prior exposure.** Age/sex adjustment does not rule out prior malaria exposure, which was not directly measured. Prior exposure could plausibly influence both baseline immune phenotype and subsequent parasite control.
4. **Title/Discussion — generalization.** A cohort recruited in Tanzania does not establish a broadly protective mechanism across African populations.

## Moderate
5. **Results/Discussion — batch residuals.** Lack of association with PC1/PC2 does not exclude batch effects in other dimensions, so “eliminated batch effects” is too broad.

**Strongest surviving conclusion:** Higher baseline CD161+ CD4 T-cell frequency was associated with delayed parasitemia in this Tanzania CHMI cohort after adjustment for age and sex.

**Top unresolved uncertainty:** Whether the baseline phenotype contributes causally to protection or instead marks prior immune exposure or another correlated state.
