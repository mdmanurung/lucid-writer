# Skeptical reviewer

## Critical
1. **Discussion — causal mechanism.** The central interpretation moves from baseline association to “mediate protection.” The Cox analysis does not establish mediation or a protective mechanism. The defensible conclusion is an association with delayed parasitemia.

## Major
2. **Discussion — IFN-gamma mechanism.** No mechanistic or perturbation experiment tested IFN-gamma. Present this as a hypothesis, not an explanation of the observed association.
3. **Discussion — prior exposure.** Adjustment for age and sex does not rule out prior malaria exposure, which was not directly measured. Prior exposure could plausibly influence both baseline phenotype and subsequent parasite control.
4. **Title/Discussion — generalization.** A cohort recruited in Tanzania does not establish a broadly protective mechanism across African populations.

**Strongest surviving conclusion:** Higher baseline CD161+ CD4 T-cell frequency was associated with delayed parasitemia in this Tanzania CHMI cohort after adjustment for age and sex.

**Top unresolved uncertainty:** Whether the phenotype contributes causally to protection or marks prior immune exposure or another correlated state.
