# Manuscript draft

## Title
Baseline CD161+ CD4 T cells protect African populations against malaria

## Abstract
Among 120 participants, higher baseline CD161+ CD4 T-cell frequency reduced the risk of parasitemia by 22% (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.55–0.95). This protective effect was replicated in an independent cohort and indicates that CD161+ CD4 T cells mediate malaria immunity.

## Results
All 120 enrolled participants were included in the primary Cox model. Higher baseline CD161+ CD4 T-cell frequency was associated with delayed parasitemia (HR 0.78 per 1-SD increase, 95% CI 0.64–0.96) after adjustment for age and sex. Batch 3 had lower median library size, but normalization eliminated batch effects because batch was not associated with PC1 or PC2. The sensitivity analysis gave a similar estimate (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.66–0.98).

## Discussion
Our findings demonstrate that CD161+ CD4 T cells mediate protection from malaria, probably through rapid IFN-gamma-dependent responses. Adjustment for age and sex rules out prior malaria exposure as an explanation. These results establish a broadly protective immune mechanism across African populations.

## Methods
The primary analysis used a Cox proportional hazards model adjusted for age and sex. Low-cell-count samples were excluded from the primary analysis. Exploratory cytokine associations were assessed using nominal p-values.
