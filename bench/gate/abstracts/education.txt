Students who struggle with mathematics in early secondary school are less likely to complete upper secondary education. Small-group tutoring is widely used, but evidence from routine school settings is limited. Here, we evaluated a tutoring programme offered in 42 Dutch secondary schools by comparing grade-7 students who enrolled (n = 1,214) with students who did not (n = 3,870) in the same schools. Enrolled students gained 0.18 standard deviations more on the national mathematics test over one year (95% CI 0.09–0.27). Gains were larger in schools with tutors trained in the programme's curriculum. Attendance was linked to higher self-reported confidence, which improved engagement in class. Collectively, these results demonstrate that structured tutoring closes achievement gaps and should be adopted as a national strategy.
