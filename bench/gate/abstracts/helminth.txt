Repeated helminth infections are common in endemic regions and are thought to dampen inflammatory responses to unrelated challenges. Dendritic cells initiate T cell responses and are known to change their activation state after chronic parasite exposure. Here, we investigated the effect of prior helminth exposure on dendritic cells during a controlled influenza vaccination by comparing previously exposed and helminth-naive adults. We observed lower levels of several type I interferon-induced chemokines in previously exposed adults, linked to changes in plasmacytoid dendritic cells. Likewise, IL-10 levels were higher in these adults and associated with altered expression of costimulatory molecules. This affected the CD80/CD86 axis, crucial for T cell priming, correlating with lower vaccine-specific antibody titres at day 28. Collectively, we present insights into how prior helminth exposure shapes dendritic cell responses to vaccination and how these, in turn, correlate with weaker humoral immunity.
