In 212 adults, higher baseline CD161+ CD4 T-cell frequency was associated with longer time to parasitaemia (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.55–0.95; p = 0.018), with persistence after adjustment for age and prior malaria episodes (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.57–0.99). Top tertile participants remained parasite-free for 41 days versus 27 days in the bottom tertile. These cells may contribute to early parasite control, although causality cannot be determined.
