# Structure

Use for framing, outlining, section design, or major restructuring.

## Governing question

For substantial text, identify:

- **Question:** what must the reader understand or decide?
- **Answer:** what is the shortest defensible answer?
- **Contribution:** what changes because this answer is supported?

A paper may contain many findings, but their relationship should not be left for the reader to infer.

## Claim hierarchy

Use a simple support tree when it clarifies the argument:

```text
governing claim
├── support claim A → evidence
├── support claim B → evidence + qualification
└── support claim C → evidence + alternative explanation
```

Vertical test: if the children are accepted, is the parent warranted?

Horizontal test: are sibling claims logically comparable and collectively useful?

Use MECE only where it is genuinely appropriate; scientific causes, mechanisms, and populations are often overlapping.

## Paragraph contract

For difficult sections, plan each paragraph as:

`function → claim → evidence → inference ceiling → bridge`

Do not make an outline a second manuscript.

## Section defaults

### Introduction
`relevant problem → unresolved gap/tension → why it matters → question/aim → contribution/approach`

Avoid textbook-scale background. The stated gap must be something the study can actually address.

### Methods
Report what was done, with the detail needed to interpret or reproduce it: design, population/material, measurement, preprocessing, analysis, quality control, exclusions, and relevant software/protocol/ethics information.

Separate planned methods from what was actually executed.

### Results
`question → analysis/comparison → observation → magnitude/uncertainty → narrow takeaway`

Keep broad mechanism, literature synthesis, and implications out unless the venue requires otherwise.

### Discussion
`answer → interpretation → relation to prior work → alternatives/mechanisms where supported → limitations → bounded implication`

A limitation should state what inference it constrains.

### Abstract
`problem/question → approach → principal results → bounded conclusion`

Do not make the abstract stronger than the body.

### Technical/scientific report
Prefer:
`answer/key finding → supporting evidence → uncertainty → implication/action → methods/detail`

Put background first only when the reader needs it to understand the answer.

### Reviewer response
For each comment:
`issue → direct answer → analysis/reasoning/change → changed location`

Disagree plainly when necessary; do not bury the answer in ceremonial gratitude.

## Structural failure modes

Flag:

- competing central claims;
- Results that do not answer a stated question;
- Discussion claims with no Results ancestor;
- Introduction promises the study cannot answer;
- evidence with no role in the argument;
- abstract/title stronger than the body;
- paragraphs that change jobs midway;
- transitions that disguise missing reasoning.
